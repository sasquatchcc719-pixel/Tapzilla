# NFC Card Printing & Manufacturing — Research

## 1. Bottom line (the 5-sentence answer)
Full-color printing is **not** what makes a card expensive — CMYK on both sides is baked into base pricing at volume, so a full-photo card costs essentially the same as a plain-logo card ([plasticcardonline.com](https://www.plasticcardonline.com/prices.htm)); what actually drives cost is the NFC chip/inlay plus the print *process* and *order volume*. A realistic **finished, printed-and-encoded** NFC card runs about **$1.05–$2.60 at 100 units, ~$0.85–$1.15 at 1,000, and ~$0.33–$0.60 at 20,000+** — notably higher than an early estimate of "$0.25–$0.80," which the verification step **contradicted** because it confused blank-plastic pricing with finished-chip-card pricing (see the flag in §4). The founder's preferred **magnet form factor is fully manufacturable** and his lived experience that "it works" is physically correct — the professional build is a layered stack (printed face → NFC inlay → **ferrite** shield → magnet), sold off-the-shelf today by [Seritag](https://seritag.com/nfc-tags/fridge-magnet-cp) and [Gyrfidstore](https://gyrfidstore.com/nfc/removable-nfc-magnets-86x54mm-work-on-metal-custom/). **No single vendor** cleanly combines US-based + blind-dropship + per-card URL encoding + a true ordering API, so the practical move is to split sourcing. **Do this first:** order a small no-MOQ test batch from [Tagstand](https://www.tagstand.com/fulfilled-by-tagstand-for-business/) (US, blind dropship, printed+encoded in USA) and get a magnet-backed sample quote from Seritag/Gyrfidstore, while pricing a bulk offset run against [ShopNFC](https://shopnfc.com/en/nfc-cards/80-offset-printed-nfc-cards.html)/GoToTags for scale.

## 2. Printing techniques — plain-English comparison

| Technique | Best for | Quality | Cost characteristic | Min quantity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Offset lithography** | Large identical runs | Excellent, full CMYK + protective overlay | Cheapest per unit at scale; high plate/setup fixed cost amortized over run | ~500–1,000 ([ShopNFC](https://shopnfc.com/en/nfc-cards/80-offset-printed-nfc-cards.html)) |
| **Digital CMYK press** | Mid runs under ~2–3k | Very good | Flat per-unit, no plates | Low / none |
| **UV inkjet flatbed** | Short, custom, made-to-order | 1200 dpi, hard scratch-resistant cured finish | No plates; flat per-unit; supports raised/texture effects | None ([Tagstand's method](https://raccoontransfers.com/blogs/guides/printing-on-pvc-cards)) |
| **Retransfer (reverse-transfer)** | 1-off to low-hundreds premium | Best desktop quality, 600 dpi, true edge-to-edge, durable film | Highest per-card (3 consumables), no plate amortization | 1–10 ([NFC.cards](https://nfc.cards/en/front-and-back-printing/61-custom-nfc-card-ntag213-front-and-back-printing.html)) |
| **Direct-to-card dye-sub** | In-house cheap runs | 300 dpi, thin white border, light varnish only | Cheapest consumables (~$0.63–$0.80/card) but least durable | 1 ([idwholesaler](https://www.idwholesaler.com/learning-center/half-panel-ymcko-ribbon/)) |
| **Silkscreen** | 1–2 color exact Pantone/metallic logos | Very durable, opaque, ink soaks in | Heavy per-color setup; volume game | High |
| **Laser engraving** | ❌ Not for PVC | Monochrome only | **Rule out** — emits toxic chlorine gas on PVC | n/a ([source](https://www.thehandsthatshape.com/blog/high-risk-materials-in-laser-cutting)) |

**Guidance:** For Tapzilla's model — one identical URL and one identical design across an entire business's order — the choice is simply volume-driven. Use **UV inkjet / digital** for launch and low-volume dropship (no plates, no MOQ, ships from the US), and switch to **offset** once a single order crosses roughly **3,000–5,000 units** (median break-even ~4,200 for CMYK card work, [source](https://customprintingco.com/blog/printing-cost-per-unit-offset-vs-digital-break-even-analysis)). Daily-carry durability comes from a real laminate film or UV-cured/offset overlay — **avoid bare direct-to-card dye-sub** ([idcardgroup](https://www.idcardgroup.com/overlaminates)).

## 3. The magnet question
**Straight answer: yes, magnet-backed NFC works, and it is a solved manufactured product — not a hack.** The verification step **confirmed** the physics on all three points ([rfidcard.com](https://www.rfidcard.com/do-magnets-affect-rfid-chips/), [eccel.co.uk](https://eccel.co.uk/how-metalwork-impacts-nfc-antenna-detuning/)):

1. **A static magnet field does NOT interfere with NFC.** NFC communicates by modulating an *oscillating* 13.56 MHz field; a permanent magnet produces a *constant (DC)* field that can't interfere with RF modulation — it only slightly raises the antenna's inductance. This is exactly why the founder's glued-on magnets read fine today.
2. **The real risk is that a magnet is a chunk of metal.** Any metal behind the coil induces eddy currents (Lenz's law) that detune the antenna, drop its Q-factor, shift resonance off 13.56 MHz, and cut read range ([leankon](https://www.leankon.com/nfc-performance-mitigation-on-metal-surfaces-technical-analysis-design-challenges-and-practical-solutions/)). A thin/weak magnet at direct-tap contact still reads; a thick/strong magnet flush against the inlay can kill it. This degrades further when the card lies flat on a **steel fridge** (the steel adds to the metal mass).
3. **The professional fix is a ferrite decoupling sheet.** Manufacturers insert a flexible ferrite layer (~0.03–0.4 mm, high permeability, low loss at 13.56 MHz) between the inlay and the magnet; it channels flux and blocks eddy currents, restoring range. The correct stack is **printed face → NFC antenna/chip → ferrite shield → magnet**.

**Proof it's a stock SKU:** Tagstand's off-the-shelf NFC fridge magnet is built exactly this way (NTAG213 + flexible ferrite sheet + PET, [source](https://www.tagstand.com/products/nfc-refrigerator-magnet-ntag213/)); [Gyrfidstore](https://gyrfidstore.com/nfc/removable-nfc-magnets-86x54mm-work-on-metal-custom/) sells a card-shaped 86×54mm removable NFC magnet with those exact layers; [Seritag](https://seritag.com/nfc-tags/fridge-magnet-cp) and [NFC Tagify](https://nfctagify.com/products/customized-nfc-magnetic-card) offer custom-printed magnet-backed NFC. Two more reassurances: everyday magnets **do not erase** NTAG memory (it's EEPROM, not magnetic stripe), and fridge-grade magnets aren't strong enough to saturate a ferrite-shielded tag ([TapTag](https://taptag.shop/blogs/how-tos/are-nfc-business-cards-or-nfc-tags-affected-by-magnets)). **Design rule for Tapzilla:** spec a ferrite layer between inlay and magnet (or an anti-metal inlay), keep the magnet thin and no stronger than needed to hold the card, and the founder's hand-glued approach becomes a repeatable manufactured SKU that reads reliably even on a steel fridge.

## 6. What to do next
1. **Order two test batches now.** (a) A ~25–50 unit no-MOQ run from **Tagstand** (US, printed+encoded, blind dropship) to validate print quality, encoding, and turnaround; (b) a magnet-backed sample from **Seritag** (MOQ 50, ferrite build) and/or a **Gyrfidstore** sample (card-shaped 86×54mm ferrite magnet) — tap-test both **on a steel fridge**, which is the real-world failure case.
2. **Get bulk quotes in parallel** from **ShopNFC** (offset, EU) and **GoToTags** (US ops/China mfg, CSV encoding) for 1k / 5k / 20k of NTAG213 CR80 cards, so you can compare landed cost (include EU/China freight + duty) against Tagstand's US quote.
3. **Spec the magnet SKU explicitly:** NTAG213 + ferrite layer between inlay and magnet + thin magnet + gloss/protective-coated face + rounded corners. Confirm read range on steel with each vendor before committing.
4. **Decide the automation path.** No vendor gives you US + blind-dropship + per-card encode + API together. Realistic options: (a) **Tagstand/TapTag portal** for US fulfillment now (manual/CSV), (b) **Shopify storefront + TapTag** if you want store integration, or (c) **Printags API** (France) if programmatic ordering becomes the top priority — accept non-US shipping.
